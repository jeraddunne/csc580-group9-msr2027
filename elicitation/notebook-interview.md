Assignment Phase B, cross-check. The same 18 questions that were put to the Google Notebook (`elicitation/google-notebook-interview.md`, the primary record) were also put to the repository's own **search notebook**, which answers only by quoting the passages that best match the question, each with a locator. It is a bounded domain-information source: not an oracle, and not a substitute for the dataset.

## 2. Protocol

1. **Scope before asking.** Each question names the tiers it may be answered from. A GitSkills question uses scope `gitskills` (A1, A2, A4, A6, A9), so it cannot be answered from the SpecMine data dictionary or the group's own notes. Process questions use `group` or `process`.
2. **Word for word, in order.** Questions are asked exactly as written in `elicitation/questions.yaml`. When an answer was incomplete, the team asked a **follow-up** with its own ID (`NB-Q05a`, `NB-Q07a`, `NB-Q11a`). Three follow-ups were added after the first pass.
3. **What the notebook returns.** The three best-matching passages (at most two per file), quoted verbatim with a locator `source · file · section or page · line`, plus a mechanical status: "match" means at least half the question's terms occur in the passages. It does **not** mean the passages answer the question. The terms no passage contains are listed, and they often point at what the sources do not establish.
4. **Verify.** Open the cited source at the locator, confirm the passage is there and says what the answer says, decide whether it answers the question, look for sources that disagree, and for claims about the data run the read-only checks V01 to V18 (`python scripts/check_interview_claims.py`). Section 4 carries the result for every answer.

**Lessons about this notebook.** It cannot invent facts, and every answer came back with a locator, so no answer needed an "unverified" label for lack of a citation. Its weakness is relevance: common words can pull passages that mention the terms without answering (NB-Q11), and it misses answers phrased differently from the question (NB-Q02 part 2, NB-Q09). Scoping every question to a tier, reading the "terms not found" line, and asking follow-ups handled all of these cases.

**Contradictions found across both interviews:** the documented `skipped_reason` is never set in the sample (V07); 13 bundled-file rows join a copy rather than a representative (V08); 16 copies carry text (V03); and the preprint's description of co-author anonymisation does not match the data or the other sources (V15).
